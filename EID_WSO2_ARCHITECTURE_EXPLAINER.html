🇸🇽 Federated eID & WSO2 IS Architectural Blueprint

A Comprehensive Technical Explainer on Boundaries, Security Protocols, and Compliance Standards for the Sint Maarten Government Cloud

🏛️ Executive Summary & Federation Topology

This document serves as the official technical architecture guide detailing the integration between **WSO2 Identity Server (IS) 7.x** and the **Sint Maarten National eID Platform (.NET 8)**.

Our architecture implements the **Federated Identity Brokerage Pattern (OIDC Federation)**. Under this design, WSO2 IS serves as the central SSO Broker and API Gateway for all relying portals (MyGov, Tax, Health, etc.), while the eID Platform serves as the **Authoritative Identity Provider (IdP)**.

💡 Key Architectural Concept: Delegated Identity Authentication

Instead of validating user passwords or OTPs directly, WSO2 IS delegates the authentication step to the eID Platform via OpenID Connect (OIDC). The eID Platform checks the user's factors (password, SMS OTP, or biometric passkey) and issues a signed cryptographically verifiable assertions token (JWT) to WSO2 IS. WSO2 IS then propagates the SSO session across all target portals.

⚖️ Resolution of Requirements Ambiguity: Why MFA Belongs to eID

During initial requirements gathering, a technical ambiguity arose regarding where the Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) engine should be hosted: within the WSO2 IS Broker or inside the eID Platform?

Strictly routing the MFA verification through WSO2 IS introduces architectural conflicts that compromise security, data privacy, and international compliance. The table below highlights the conflicts of the naive approach vs. the security of our implemented solution:

eIDAS High Assurance (Level High)

The Conflict: For the eID Platform to sign digital transactions at "Level High" compliance, it must verify multiple factors directly. If WSO2 IS handled the MFA step, the eID platform would only check the user's password, generating a "Low Assurance" token.
The Solution: The eID Platform executes the full multi-factor authentication (Password + OTP/Passkey) and signs the assertions, satisfying eIDAS regulations.

FIDO2 / WebAuthn Domain Bindings

The Conflict: WebAuthn/Passkeys are browser-level credentials bound to a specific origin (domain). Because the citizen registers their biometrics on the eID portal domain (`http://localhost:3000`), WSO2 IS (`https://localhost:9443`) cannot verify the cryptographic biometric signature due to browser cross-origin boundaries.
The Solution: By putting the WebAuthn challenge engine inside the eID Platform, biometrics are checked on the correct origin seamlessly.

GDPR Data Minimization & PII Isolation

The Conflict: If WSO2 IS directly sent SMS/Email OTP codes, it would require access to the master citizen database containing phone numbers and email addresses. This would necessitate replicating sensitive PII database tables into WSO2's repository, creating an audit vulnerability and a wider attack surface.
The Solution: eID encapsulates the citizen database. WSO2 IS acts under "Zero-Knowledge" of phone numbers/secrets, receiving only a final token mapping the claims.

⚖️ Architectural Boundary Allocation Matrix

🛡️ eID Platform (.NET 8 Backend)

The primary identity verification and cryptography engine:

  • Credential Verification: Direct validation of passwords (via BCrypt verification) and biometric public keys.
  • MFA Engine: Generates and verifies 6-digit Email/SMS OTPs, and houses the RFC 6238 TOTP verification (Google Authenticator) algorithms.
  • Database Authority: Serves as the master registry of citizen profiles (UN/CEFACT Compliant).
  • Cryptographic Evidence Logging: Generates and writes SHA-256 digital digests of transactions (Annex E Logging) in DB.

⚙️ WSO2 Identity Server (SSO Broker)

The primary access control policy and protocol mediator:

  • Access Policy Enforcement: Decides which portals require multi-factor verification (MFA step-up) based on risk tier.
  • Protocol Translation: Mediates SAML 2.0 / WS-Fed / OIDC callbacks between client portals and eID.
  • SSO Session Management: Maintains global session cookies, single-logout redirects, and tokens lifecycle.
  • Shadow Profiles & Claims Filtering: Filters and releases only the claims authorized for a specific portal, keeping other profile attributes private.
